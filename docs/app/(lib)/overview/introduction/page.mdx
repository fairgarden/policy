# How the policy works

A community's rules — who may join, who may vote, what is shared and with
whom — are usually scattered through code as `if` statements: hard to find,
harder to review, and invisible to the people they apply to.

This package
puts them in one policy the organization owns.

## Who writes what

- **Each service** names the decisions it makes and asks them at the right
  moment, with the evidence it holds: the members service asks `admit` when
  someone wants to join, with what the id service vouches for about them and
  who referred them. It ships [built-in rules](/overview/decisions), and the
  same rules in Rego with places for an organization to add its own.
- **The organization** writes its rules on top: its bylaws, its privacy
  policy. They live in the distribution's `policies/`, beside `apps/` and
  `packages/`, and change the way the rest of the repository does — by pull
  request, reviewed by the people responsible.

See [Layers](/overview/layers) for how the two fit, and
[Bylaws as policy](/overview/bylaws) for what an organization writes.

## How it reaches the services

The policy is built from the repository when the distribution is deployed —
once, however many services run it — and each service runs its copy in its
own process. There is no workflow to run and nothing to publish: what is in
the repository is what runs. See [Deploying](/overview/deploying).

## Decisions people can see

A decision that says no says why, in words the person it is about is shown:
the members service lists what an applicant must still do, and the id service
shows each thing it will not offer a service, and why. The id service also
shows the whole policy at `/policy` — what each decision is for, in the
organization's words, and every rule — so anyone can read what governs them.

## Every decision recorded

Each decision is logged in the format OPA logs its own, labelled with the
[revision](/overview/decision-log#revisions) of the policy that made it, and
each revision is kept. So any decision, however old, can be read beside the
exact rules that made it.
